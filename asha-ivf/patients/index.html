Patient guide

Your embryo's big day

Today your care team may warm your frozen embryo and watch how it wakes up. This guide shows what is happening, step by step, in plain words. Tap, slide and flip things as you go.

1 · Growing

From one tiny cell to a blastocyst

An embryo grows for about five to six days in the lab incubator. Pick a day and watch the cells multiply.

2 · Freeze and warm

A pause button, then a gentle wake-up

Blastocysts can be frozen very quickly and kept safe until you are ready. On transfer day we warm one and give it time to wake up. Tap each step.

3 · Our AI helper

A second pair of eyes, never the decision-maker

While your embryo recovers, our embryologists take a few photos. A computer program then looks at those photos and gives a score from 0 to 10.

Photos

Photos are taken while your embryo wakes up.

AI score

The program gives one number from 0 to 10.

Your team

Your doctor and embryologist weigh the score together with what they see and with you.

The score is one clue, like a weather forecast. It does not tell us what will happen, and it never decides for you.

4 · Your choices

Two paths, and you are part of the choice

Sometimes the team asks: shall we go ahead with this embryo, or warm another one too? Either can be a good, caring choice. Your doctor will talk it through with you.

Path A

Go ahead with this embryo

  • We continue today as planned.
  • No other embryo needs to be warmed.
  • Your doctor explains what the photos and score mean for your case.
Path B

Warm one more embryo

  • Only possible if you have another frozen embryo.
  • Gives the team another embryo to look at.
  • Not every embryo wakes up after warming, so we talk about this first.
